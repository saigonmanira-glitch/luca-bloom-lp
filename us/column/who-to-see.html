Who to see about your foreskin, and when

October 5, 2026

Your foreskin feels tight, or odor or itching bothers you, yet you do not know who to see or whether it is “worth” it, so you leave it. Many people do. This article covers who to talk to and when.

Key points

  • Start with a primary care doctor; they can refer you to a urologist
  • For skin problems such as redness or itching, a dermatologist may also help
  • Will not go back or sudden swelling: contact a medical service straight away

Start with a primary care doctor

For questions about the foreskin, a primary care doctor is the usual first step. They can look at it, explain what to watch out for, and refer you to a urologist if needed. Urologists specialize in the urinary tract (the kidneys, bladder and urethra) and the male genitals.

Doctors see people of every age about this. Asking about your foreskin is nothing unusual.

When a dermatologist may help

If the main problem is on the skin surface, such as redness, itching, eczema or raw skin, a dermatologist may also help. Tightness, how far it draws back and anything to do with passing urine are for a primary care doctor or a urologist. If in doubt, start with a primary care doctor.

WhoTypical questions
Primary care doctorThe first person to ask about anything; can refer you
UrologistTightness, how far it draws back, passing urine, the foreskin in general
DermatologistRedness, itching, eczema, raw skin and other skin-surface problems

When to get advice

Consider getting advice if any of the following applies.

  • It hurts to draw the foreskin back, or you cannot draw it back
  • Redness, swelling or itching that continues or keeps coming back
  • A pus-like discharge from inside the foreskin
  • Strong odor that bothers you even with careful washing
  • The foreskin balloons when you pass urine, or urine is slow to come out
  • Split skin or bleeding
  • You simply want to know properly where you stand

The last one on its own is reason enough. Having a professional take a look brings peace of mind faster than worrying over what you read online.

When to contact a medical service straight away

If the foreskin has tightened behind the head and will not go back, the head or foreskin suddenly swells, or the pain is severe, do not wait: contact a medical service straight away. In an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

What to prepare

  • When it started and what bothers you
  • The specific problem: pain, itching, odor and so on
  • How you usually wash and anything you have tried
  • Any ID, health card or insurance details your practice asks for

So that nerves do not make you forget anything, it helps to write it down on your phone beforehand.

Finding a practice

Look on the practice website for opening hours, how to book and what they can help with. When booking, you do not need to go into detail; “I would like advice about my foreskin” is enough.

During the appointment, it is also worth asking about washing and everyday care. You will get advice that fits your own situation, which makes daily life simpler afterwards.

Summary

For questions about the foreskin, start with a primary care doctor, who can refer you to a urologist. For skin problems, a dermatologist may help too. If it will not go back or suddenly swells, contact a medical service straight away. Do not hold back thinking it is “only a small thing”: asking when it first bothers you gives the most peace of mind.

This article is general information, not medical advice or a diagnosis. If you have pain, swelling, bleeding or difficulty passing urine, talk to a doctor.
