Washing and changing at the gym or pool: simple habits

October 6, 2026

Showering and changing in a shared space is part of going to the gym or the pool. A few simple habits can make washing and getting dressed straightforward. This article covers what to pack, how to wash gently and how to dry off. It also explains when to talk to a doctor.

Key points

  • Rinse with lukewarm water and a mild soap
  • Pat dry and change out of wet clothes soon
  • See a doctor about pain, tightness or swelling

Before anything else: when to get advice

If something about your foreskin feels physically wrong, talk to a doctor. Do not just plan around it. Pain, tightness, swelling, soreness or a change you have not noticed before all deserve a proper conversation with a family doctor.

The changing room is not the place to work out what is going on. A short appointment is the right step. The full list of signs to look out for is further down this page.

What to pack in your bag

Packing the same things each time keeps your routine simple. You will not need to borrow anything or rush.

  • A mild, unscented soap or body wash in a small bottle
  • Two towels, one for your body and a smaller one for drying carefully
  • Clean, breathable cotton underwear
  • A separate waterproof bag for wet swimwear or sweaty clothes
  • Flip-flops or shower shoes for wet floors
  • A comb, deodorant or anything else you use every day

Keeping wet and dry items apart means your clean clothes stay clean. It also means you are not putting fresh underwear into a damp bag.

Washing after swimming or exercise

After a swim or a workout, a quick shower rinses off pool water and sweat. You do not need anything special. Keep it gentle and simple.

A simple shower routine

  1. Set the water to lukewarm, not hot.
  2. Rinse your whole body first to wash off pool water or sweat.
  3. Use a small amount of mild soap on the outside of the genital area.
  4. If your foreskin draws back easily and comfortably, ease it back a little and rinse with water only.
  5. Do not force it, and stop if anything feels uncomfortable.
  6. Rinse away all the soap, then return the foreskin to its usual position.
  7. Finish with a final rinse of lukewarm water.

Strong or scented products are not needed for this area. Plain water and a mild soap on the outside are enough for everyday washing.

If you prefer, you can do a quick rinse at the venue and a fuller wash at home. Both are fine.

Drying and getting dressed

Taking a moment to dry properly is a useful habit. Damp skin under clothing can feel unpleasant for the rest of the day.

  • Pat the area dry with a clean towel rather than rubbing
  • Give the skin a moment in the air before dressing, if you can
  • Put on clean, dry cotton underwear
  • Change out of wet swimwear soon after leaving the water
  • Wash your gym kit and towels regularly

Practical options for privacy

If you would like more privacy while changing, there are simple practical choices. Many venues have individual cubicles or shower stalls with a door. You can ask the staff what is available. A large towel or a changing robe lets you dress while covered. Some people simply shower and change at home if they live nearby.

These are just options. Use whichever suits your routine.

When to talk to a doctor

Talk to a family doctor if you notice any of the following. You do not need to wait for it to get worse.

  • Your foreskin feels tight, or drawing it back is painful
  • Redness, soreness, itching or swelling that does not settle
  • Any discharge, bleeding or a strong smell that is new for you
  • Pain when passing urine
  • A lump, sore or skin change you have not noticed before
  • Anything that worries you about the area

If you are receiving treatment for the area, follow the advice of your family doctor and ask them before changing your routine.

If your foreskin has been pulled back and will not return to its usual position, or there is severe pain or swelling, get medical help straight away. Do not wait for an appointment. In an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department. For health advice, most provinces offer 811.

Summary

Pack a mild soap, two towels, clean cotton underwear and a bag for wet items. After swimming or exercise, rinse with lukewarm water and use a little mild soap on the outside only. Never force the foreskin back. Pat dry and change out of wet clothes soon. Cubicles, towels or a changing robe are practical options if you would like more privacy. If anything feels painful, tight, swollen or unusual, talk to a family doctor.

This article is general information, not medical advice or a diagnosis. If you have pain, swelling, bleeding or difficulty passing urine, talk to a doctor.
