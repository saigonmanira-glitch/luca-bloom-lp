Odour under the foreskin: washing, underwear and daily habits

5 October 2026

Many people worry about odour under the foreskin. It is hard to judge yourself, and easy to worry about whether others notice. This article looks at why odour builds up and what you can check in your daily routine.

Key points

  • Wash gently inside the foreskin, as far as is comfortable
  • Do not over-wash; keep the area dry
  • Dab away any urine after using the toilet

Why odour builds up

Skin oil, sweat, old skin cells and traces of urine collect easily on the inside of the foreskin and can form a white build-up (smegma). The covered area also tends to stay damp, and build-up left there causes odour. The narrower the opening and the harder it is to wash inside, the more noticeable odour tends to be.

Washing

Wash inside the foreskin, as far as is comfortable

In the shower or bath, draw the foreskin back only as far as is painless and wash gently with your fingertips. No force is needed. Use a little well-lathered mild soap and rinse thoroughly. Afterwards, return the foreskin to its usual position.

Do not over-wash

Worrying about odour makes you want to wash again and again. But over-washing dries and irritates the skin and can make you less comfortable. Once a day, when you shower or bathe, is enough.

Underwear

  • Choose fabrics that absorb sweat, such as cotton
  • Pick a size that is not too tight
  • Change on days when you sweat, if you can
  • Make sure washed underwear is completely dry before wearing it

When the area stays damp, odour is trapped. Paying attention to airflow makes a real difference to comfort.

One extra step after the toilet

A little urine can remain at the tip of the foreskin. Getting into the habit of dabbing it away with toilet paper removes one cause of odour.

After sweating

After exercise or on hot days, rinse off in the shower, or if that is not possible, just change your underwear. The point is not to stay damp for long.

Do not mask it with scent

Covering odour with perfume or strongly scented sprays can irritate the skin, and mixed smells can be more noticeable. Cleanliness and dryness come first.

When to talk to a doctor

If the odour stays strong even with careful washing, or there is redness, itching, pain or a pus-like discharge, skin inflammation may be involved. Do not rely on washing alone: talk to a doctor.

Not letting it take over

Others often notice odour far less than you fear. Once you start worrying about it, it can be on your mind whatever you do, and that in itself is stressful.

Once you have tried the washing, underwear and habit changes above, give yourself credit for doing what you can. If it still bothers you, asking a doctor for an objective view is the most reassuring step.

If a partner or family member mentions it, there is no need to blame yourself. Keep up your routine and ask about anything that worries you early.

Summary

The basics are washing inside the foreskin as far as is comfortable, not over-washing, avoiding dampness and dabbing away urine after the toilet. If it still bothers you, talk to a doctor rather than worrying alone.

This article is general information, not medical advice or a diagnosis. If you have pain, swelling, bleeding or difficulty passing urine, talk to a doctor.
